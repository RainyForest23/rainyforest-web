Tesla Jr.: a car that drives on cameras alone

01

Decision [Draft]

Whose decision does this help?

The decision is a build decision: can a small car be driven with cameras only, or does it need a LiDAR and a map?

It is my own decision, for the next step. A real ride-on car (a Henes Broon T870) is waiting for whatever works here, and cameras are the cheap option.

So the question for the simulator was narrow: does a camera-only, map-free stack get a car-shaped vehicle to a goal past obstacles?

02

Before [Draft]

How was it done before, in numbers?

The usual course project looks like this:

  • a two-wheel base that can turn on the spot,
  • a LiDAR,
  • a map made beforehand with SLAM,
  • a stock robot in Gazebo.

Each of those makes navigation easier. A car cannot turn on the spot, a camera gives depth only where it looks, and with no map there is nothing to plan on except what has been seen so far.

03

Ontology [Draft]

What are the objects, how are they related, what can be done to them?

Vehicle
Steers with its front wheels. Wheelbase 0.65 m, wheel radius 0.12 m, footprint 1.0 × 0.6 m, tightest turn 1.0 m.
Camera
A stereo pair facing forward, later one more on each side, together covering about 180° ahead. Each gives a depth image.
Point
A place where the camera saw something: 307,200 of them per frame, about 27 frames a second.
Costmap cell
A small square of floor, free or blocked. The near map rolls along with the car; nothing is stored between runs.
Goal
A position to reach, within a tolerance of 0.35 m.
Path
A curve from the car to the goal that a car can actually follow.
Command
A forward speed and a turn rate, sent many times a second.
Detection
A person found in the camera image by YOLO.

Actions: see (camera to points), mark (points to cells), plan (cells and goal to path), follow (path to command), convert (command to steering angle and wheel speed), stop (a person ahead).

04

Smallest version [Draft]

What was the smallest version that worked, and what were the sub-problems?

The smallest version was not a car. It was the simulator's stock robot driven by keyboard, with one question: do camera images and points arrive on the other side at all?

  • Drive a car to a goal on cameras alone
    • Get data across
      • Images at about 30 a second, points at about 27
    • See obstacles
      • Points into costmap cells
    • Model the car
      • My own T870 model, steering and drive joints
    • Turn commands into wheel motion
      • The conversion node, which also reports the car's position
    • Plan for a car
      • A planner and controller that do not ask it to spin
    • Check it
      • A straight goal, then obstacles, a gate, a corner, a maze

Only after the stock robot worked end to end was it swapped for the car.

05

Trade-offs [Draft]

What were the options, and why this one?

Car steering, not a two-wheel base
Harder to plan for, and the point of the project: the real vehicle is a car.
Cameras, no LiDAR
Cheaper, and blind wherever no camera points. Side cameras were added for that; there is still none at the back.
No map
Nothing to prepare and nothing to go stale. The cost is that the car only knows what it has seen, which is what stopped it in the maze.
Planner: the default, then Hybrid-A*
The default planner asked for turns a car cannot make. Hybrid-A* plans with the car's turning circle.
Controller: three tries
The default one turns on the spot. The second followed curves but hugged walls in a corner. The third (MPPI) checks the car's real outline and may reverse.
Depth from the simulator
The depth image is the simulator's exact one, a stand-in for real stereo matching. It kept the project about navigation. It is also the largest gap to the real car.
One node owns the car
Driving the wheels and reporting position are done by one node, because two programs holding the same simulated car broke each other.
06

What broke [Draft]

What failed, and how was it fixed?

The floor looked like a wall
Floor points were marked as obstacles: 2,243 of 6,400 cells. A goal 2 m away came back as a 14 m path and the car stood still. The floor appeared to rise with distance and crossed the 0.15 m line. Raising it to 0.35 m fixed it.
The wheels spun in place
The rear wheels turned at the right speed and the car barely moved. The simulator had turned each round wheel into a rough many-sided shape. Giving the wheels a sphere to roll on fixed it.
Driving stopped when position reporting started
Two scripts each took hold of the simulated car and cancelled each other's hold. They became one node.
Data that was listed but never arrived
The two sides of the link used different versions of the same transport and failed silently over shared memory. Forcing plain network transport fixed it.
Points that would not show
The points and the car's position were stamped by two different clocks. Everything was moved to one clock.
07

Measured [Draft]

What was the result, and how do we know?

All of this is in simulation.

TestResult
Goal 11 m ahead, two obstaclesReached. Swerved 0.13 m right, then 0.12 m left, stopped at 10.81 m (tolerance 0.35 m)
Posts outside the front camera's viewPassed, once the side cameras were added
A 2.4 m wide cornerDriven without touching the walls
A person aheadDetected at 0.82 confidence, about 18 frames a second; the car stops and goes on
A closed maze with four cornersFailed after the first corner

The maze is the honest limit. With no rear camera the car could not see the wall behind it when backing up. A fallback was added: if the car is told to move and does not, it assumes something is there and marks it. That stopped it from cheating around the outside, and did not get it through.

The conclusion written down at the time: a maze like that needs a map.

08

Next [Draft]

What is the next version?

  • Real stereo matching in place of the simulator's depth.
  • The same stack on the real car: a Jetson for the computing, motor drivers for steering and drive.
  • A language model choosing the goal. The small node that accepts a goal as text is already there.

None of these is done yet.

My part. Everything in the project: the simulation, the car model, the three nodes, the navigation set-up and the tests. As the repository says, an AI coding assistant helped with environment set-up, debugging and documentation.
