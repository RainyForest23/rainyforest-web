A car, a camera, no map.

Course robots usually turn on the spot, carry a LiDAR and drive on a map made beforehand.This one steers like a car, sees only through cameras, and has no map.Built and tested in a simulator, as a one-person course project.

See.

A stereo camera on the nose gives depth: 307,200 points, about 27 times a second.Each point is a place where something is.

The floor looked like a wall.

At first the floor itself was marked as an obstacle: 2,243 of 6,400 cells.A goal 2 m away came back as a 14 m path, and the car did not move.Raising the height that counts as an obstacle from 0.15 m to 0.35 m left only the real ones.

Think: a path a car can drive.

The first planner turned too sharply for a car that cannot turn on the spot.A planner that knows the car's turning circle, 1.0 m, draws a gentle S instead.

Drive: from a command to the wheels.

The planner asks for a speed and a turn rate.A car has neither. It has a steering angle, and wheels that spin.The node written for this project turns one into the other.

And round again.

It swerves 0.13 m right for the first obstacle and 0.12 m left for the second, and stops at 10.81 m, inside the 0.35 m tolerance.See, think, drive: about 20 times a second.
