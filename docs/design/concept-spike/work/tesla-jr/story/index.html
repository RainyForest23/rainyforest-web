A car, a camera, no map.

Course robots usually turn on the spot, carry a LiDAR and drive on a map made beforehand.This one steers like a car, sees only through cameras, and has no map.Built and tested in a simulator, as a one-person course project.

See: depth becomes points.

Three stereo cameras cover about 180° ahead.Each depth image becomes a cloud of points: 307,200 from the front camera, about 27 times a second.A point is a place where something is.

Mark: points become a costmap.

Nav2 drops the points into a grid. At first the floor itself counted: 2,243 of 6,400 cells, and the car stood still.Only points above a height now count. Raising that cut from 0.15 m to 0.35 m left the real obstacles.Then each obstacle is given a margin, 0.55 m wide. The closer to it, the higher the cost.

Plan: search only what a car can drive.

The first planner drew corners a car cannot take.Hybrid-A* searches differently. From each position it tries three short arcs: left at the tightest turn, 1.0 m, straight, and right.Arcs that run into cost are dropped. What is left is a path the car can follow.

Follow: imagine many futures, take the cheapest.

Twenty times a second the controller rolls 1,000 possible next two seconds through a model of the car.Each is scored: stay near the path, stay out of cost, do not hit anything with the car's real outline.The cheapest one gives the next command: a speed and a turn rate.

Drive: from a command to the wheels.

A car has no turn rate. It has a steering angle, and wheels that spin.The node written for this project turns one into the other.It also reports where the car now is, which starts the loop again.

Look again: the plan changes.

Nothing is planned once. The costmap only marks what a camera has seen within 6 m, so the second obstacle is not in the first plan.A few metres on it comes into range: new points, new cells, a new search, a new path.All the while the controller keeps taking the cheapest of its futures. The car swerves 0.13 m right, then 0.12 m left, and stops at 10.81 m.

No eyes at the back.

The cameras look forward, left and right. Backing out of a dead end, the car could not see the wall behind it and kept reversing into it.The fix is a bumper made of logic: told to move, not moving, for a second, so something must be there.That place is marked in the costmap like anything a camera saw. The wheels stop and the planner looks for another way.
